Human Factors (C8):
Automation Bias: People gave significant weight to computer-generated figures, treating them as more trustworthy than human explanations.
Lack of Trust in Users: Sub-postmasters' complaints were ignored rather than being treated as valuable evidence of wider system problems.
Pressure & Communication: Users were pressured to repay shortfalls immediately, making it difficult to challenge the system, while technical problems were not clearly communicated to decision-makers.

Management Decisions & Ethics (C10):
Management continued to rely on Horizon data despite concerns, failed to conduct independent investigations before legal action, and prioritized defending the organization over objective facts.
Engineering & Ethics Analysis
Public Interest & Avoiding Harm: Professionals failed to consider the severe financial and legal impact on sub-postmasters and ignored known risks that caused harm.
Honesty and Transparency: Information about bugs and system limitations was not communicated clearly to decision-makers.
Duty to Escalate: Individuals aware of the risks failed their responsibility to raise concerns to appropriate decision-makers or utilize whistleblowing channels.
Professional Responsibility: The ACM/IEEE Software Engineering Code of Ethics was breached, specifically regarding the principles of acting in the public interest, avoiding harm, and reporting serious risks.
