Timeline of the Horizon Failure:

1999: Horizon software is deployed to 18,000 Post Office branches. Immediate reports of accounting discrepancies begin.
2000–2010: The Post Office aggressively prosecutes sub-postmasters for shortfalls generated by the software, relying on flawed data as infallible evidence.
2009: Computer Weekly publishes the first major technical exposé of the Horizon bugs.
2012: Forensic accountants from Second Sight are appointed to investigate but are actively hindered by Post Office management withholding data.
2015: The Post Office halts criminal prosecutions but continues to defend the software's integrity.
2019: In Bates v Post Office, High Court Justice Fraser officially rules that the Horizon system contained "bugs, errors and defects" that caused the shortfalls, comprehensively defeating the Post Office's technical defense.
2020–Present: Massive public inquiries are launched, and hundreds of criminal convictions are progressively quashed.
